Accounts

What account types does RustToGold show?

RustToGold separates accounts into Everyday Savings, Fixed Deposit Account, and Investment Account. Everyday Savings is the main operating account. Fixed Deposit Account is for term deposits. Investment Account is a future-ready product area.

Can a customer hold more than one account?

Yes. A realistic banking system should allow one customer to hold multiple accounts, such as one savings account and one or more fixed deposit records linked to it.

Cards

Why are cards separated from features?

Cards are better treated as product lines because debit, credit, and student cards have different eligibility, limits, and account linkage rules.

Can cards be frozen?

Yes. The security page includes card freeze and unfreeze as customer-visible controls. The dashboard can show card status clearly.

Money Transfers

What should the transfer flow include?

A strong transfer flow includes source account, recipient, amount, remarks, confirmation, OTP for risky actions, and a receipt saved into transaction history.

When should OTP be required?

OTP can be required for high-value transfers, new recipients, repeated unusual transfers, or other actions your business rules mark as sensitive.

Fixed Deposits & Loans

What is the difference between Fixed Deposit Account and Fixed Deposits?

The Fixed Deposit Account is the product category. The Fixed Deposits section explains the actual workflow: placement amount, tenure, interest, maturity date, and payout instruction.

What should loan management show?

Loan management should show application details, status, approval decision, disbursement account, repayment summary, and loan history.

Security

Is RustToGold a real bank?

No. RustToGold is an academic banking simulation. The content is designed to feel realistic while remaining clearly framed as a project.

What security features should be shown?

Show login protection, OTP verification, card freeze controls, activity alerts, suspicious activity review, role-based access, and audit logging.
